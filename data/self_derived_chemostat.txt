BACTERIAL CHEMOSTAT COMPETITION: MECHANISM DESCRIPTION

Two bacterial strains, A (fast-growing, but sensitive to the limiting
substrate) and B (slow-growing, but tolerant of low substrate), are grown
together in a continuously stirred chemostat.

The chemostat supplies fresh medium containing a single limiting nutrient S
at constant concentration, and culture is withdrawn at a constant dilution
rate D, so that both microbes and the nutrient are continuously removed at
that same rate.

Biological mechanisms described in the paper:

- The nutrient S is consumed by both strains as they grow.
- Strain A grows following a Monod (saturating) relationship with the
  nutrient: its specific growth rate rises with S but saturates at a maximum.
- Strain B also follows a Monod relationship, but with a different half
  saturation constant and maximum rate.
- Each strain's population in the vessel changes because of growth plus the
  continuous washout at dilution rate D.
- The nutrient concentration changes because of inflow of fresh medium,
  consumption by both strains, and washout.
- To allow coexistence, the strains must differ in their Monod parameters;
  otherwise one outcompetes the other.

The paper does NOT write down explicit equations, but it states that the
system is fully deterministic and that under certain parameter regimes both
strains coexist at a stable steady state in the nutrient-limited chemostat,
while under other regimes only one strain survives.

Task for the modelling agent: reconstruct a system of ordinary differential
equations describing the chemostat competition mechanism, justify each term
biologically, and explore whether coexistence is possible for well-chosen
parameters.
